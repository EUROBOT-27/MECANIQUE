 A. NOTICE

The rules have been split up in severals documents. Most parts remain the same for the Eurobot Open and
Eurobot Open Junior contests, but to avoid confusion, each contest has its own rules as a single document.
Thus the particular cases specific to one of the competitions appears only in the document concerning it.
You will find the Eurobot Open and the Eurobot Open Junior rules and other information on the Eurobot Open
website (www.eurobot.org/)

 Please note that the version of this release is noted down at the end of this page. For any inquiry, only
 an OFFICIAL version should be considered.

Changes or clarifications of the rules may be made during the year. We therefore strongly encourage all par-
ticipants to check our website regularly (www.eurobot.org/) as well as your NOC’s own website for news. You
can also follow discussions, ask questions or get further assistance on our faq (www.eurobot.org/faq/).
Any changes to the specifications will, if necessary, be set out in a supplementary document which will be
available on the website of your national organising committee1 and Eurobot (https://www.eurobot.org/).
The FAQ responses from a referee are official responses taken into account for match refereeing and homolo-
gation stages.
In case of doubt regarding any point of the rules or the homologation of robots, the referee committee may also
be contacted at referee@planete-sciences.org.

Have a good reading!

 As is the case every year, a number of parameters in these rules have been modified. As a result,
 please reread in detail all the parts of these rules, even those that seem familiar to you.

 B. CONTEST PRESENTATION

EurobotOpen and EurobotOpen Junior are two international amateur robotics competitions for young people or-
ganised in clubs, groups of friends or schools. Their common aim is to enable young people to take charge of
their own learning and put their knowledge, technical and social skills into practice, by taking part in a fun and
friendly event. The technical challenge involves building one or more robots.

EurobotOpen and EurobotOpen Junior have for only ambitions those described above. As an actor of the popular
education mouvement and as a youth movement, inclusiveness is a strong value for Planète Sciences and its
European partners. The organizers do not (and will never) impose a team model; whether you participate to
discover robotics, to practice your skills, as part of teaching or for competition, you are (and always will be)
welcome.

The EurobotOpen and EurobotOpen Junior meetings are prepared passionately throughout the year by volunteers
of all nationalities who believe in the educational values of this experience and are themselves, often, former
participants.

The rules for both contest, EurobotOpen and EurobotOpen Junior, are based on the same concept. As or-
ganisers, we intend to provide a common platform for the EurobotOpen event. This platform is dedicated to
autonomous robots for EurobotOpen while for EurobotOpen Junior, the robots are remote controlled. In this way,
the EurobotOpen organiser can easily set up the Eurobot Junior contest and vice versa.

 C. THEME PRESENTATION

Hear ye, hear ye, good people, ladies and gentlemen! Let me tell you the story of the great King Arthur, the
quest for the Grail, and the legendary Camelot.
Once upon a time, in the land of robots who fancied themselves knights, there lived a robot named Arthur who
set off on a quest for the Holy Grail. This wasn’t as difficult as all that, according to an old robot he met in a
tavern, who had over-recharged his batteries and was carrying a bag full of Grails.
But the king also learned that another robot was claiming to be King Arthur and wanted to steal his precious
Grail, so there was no time to lose; he had to be safely secured behind the walls of Camelot!

Your feats of arms will be:

       Build the glorious Camelot.
       I have the real one!
       The return of the king.
       To the attack!

 Please note: All actions are independent from one another and no specific sequence is imposed what-
 soever. No single action is mandatory. Give careful thoughts to your strategy. It is strongly recom-
 mended to design simple and reliable systems with a limited number of actions.

  D.1. IMPORTANT INFORMATION
The organizers are committed to build the playing area with as much accuracy as possible. Nevertheless,
minor tolerances may be observed depending on manufacturing constraints.

                    No complaints regarding dimensional deviations will be taken into account.

Teams are warned that the surface condition may differ from one playing area to another and may also degrade
over time.
Graphics displayed in this document represent the EurobotOpen playing area and not the EurobotOpen Junior
one. Thus figures show elements not useful in this version of the rules such as fixed beacon supports and
remote computing device.

  D.2. PLAYING AREA

The playing area is a horizontal rectangular plane of 3000 mm by 2000 mm with 70 mm heigh, 22 mm thick
borders on each outer side. Depending on the construction process, it may consist of one or several parts
(eg 3 parts of 1000 mm per 2000 mm). Please note that the joints may have imperfections, and it will not be
possible to contest these imperfections during the competition. The organization reserves the right to set up
devices for recording (camera) on the unused edges of the playing area.

Figure 2: Detailed view of the playing area, audience point of view

1. The throne room (Robot starting areas)  4. Ramparts
2. Castle courtyard                        5. Moat
3. Stables (SIMA Starting Areas)           6. Stone quarry

Full specifications of the playing area and game elements (dimensions, positions at the beginning of
the match, colors and other references) are listed in the appendix.
In the remainder of this document, horizontal and vertical directions are stated relative to the playing
area. Notions of “left”, “right”, “front” and “back” are stated with respect to the spectator’s point of
view.

  D.3. STARTING AREAS

D.3.a. DESCRIPTION

Each team has one starting and finishing area.
The "throne rooms" are the team’s starting and finishing areas. They are the surfaces of 50 cm by 50 cm
delimited by a line on the table, which is itself included in this area and of the team’s color; they are placed at
the side of the table.

(a) Team blue starting and finishing area  (b) Team yellow starting and finishing area

Figure 3: Detailed view of the blue and yellow teams areas

D.3.b. CONSTRAINTS
At the end of the setup time, the vertical projection of the robots must not exceed the limits of their starting area.

Make sure your robot can fully enter its starting area.
The colored lines and the border of the table next to the area are also included in the starting area.

  D.4. DESCRIPTION AND LAYOUT OF THE PLAYING ELEMENTS

 The stone: The stones are cardboard boxes measuring 320 mm x 110 mm x 110 mm.
There are 30 of them, and they are placed in the stone quarries.
An Aruco tag with a value of 13 is present on each of the 4 large faces of the stone, oriented as shown in the
photo in the description.

 Stone quarries: Zones distributed across the table. They contain 3 stones in a horizontal position, stacked
one on top of the other.

  Rampart: Construction zones are distributed on each side of the table and reserved for each team (see
distribution on the table plan), delimited by a line which is itself included within these zones. There are 2 types
of rampart: walls with a rectangular surface of 200 mm by 400 mm, and towers with a circular surface and a
diameter of 200 mm.

 Castle courtyard: Areas placed on the sides of the table and surrounded by ramparts, it’s includ the throne
room.

  Moat: Areas spread across the table and surrounding the ramparts, delimited by a line which is itself
included within these areas.

 The knights: Built by the team, a knight is a small independent mobile actuator (SIMA) activated during
the match. It will initially be placed in the SIMA starting areas (stable) with a rectangular surface of 200 mm
by 300 mm. For ease of identification, a harmonization of design and colors between the SIMA and the robot
is desired.

  The Grail: Made by the team, the Grail is an element placed on the throne room of the team before the
match.

  The cannonballs: Each team has 10 lightweight foam balls, each ball being 45mm in diameter and are
compressible.

 E. ACTIONS

  E.1. CONSTRUCTION OF THE GLORIOUS CAMELOT!

To keep the Grail safe, we must build an impregnable castle, the legendary Camelot. But be warned, for the
building to withstand the test of time, we must strictly adhere to the plans of the chief architect.

E.1.a. DESCRIPTION AND LAYOUT OF THE PLAYING ELEMENTS
This action involves the stones and the ramparts.

E.1.b. ACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS
 Actions:

       The goal is to collect the stones and place them within the ramparts, carefully following the castle’s layout,
       between the walls, towers, and castle gate.

 Constraints:

       A stone is considered placed in a zone if all or part of its vertical projection is in this zone, or on top of a
       stone already valid for that zone.
       A wall is made up of 3 stones arranged horizontally and stacked one on top of the other. A wall must be
       built within a rectangular wall area.
       A tower is composed of 1 stone placed vertically. A tower must be built within a circular area.
       A gate consists of 2 stones placed vertically and topped with a third stone placed horizontally. A gate
       must be built within a rectangular area of the wall.
       Game elements are permitted to exceed the allowed height limit but only within construction zones and
       the castle boundaries. Please note that the actuators must respect the height limit at all times during the
       match.
       Each zone can only contain one building of the type permitted for that zone, and a castle can only have
       one gate. Additional constructions will not award points related to the construction, however, points for
       stones placed will still be counted.
       A stone cannot be counted for multiple constructions at the same time.
       An element still under control by a robot at the end of the match will not be counted.
       During the match, it will be possible to store up to 3 stones in the castle courtyard.
       During the match, it will be possible to steal stones from the opposing castle to build your castle, but
       only if all the stone quarries are empty, and without only crash the opposing castle. A minimum 50-point
       penalty for unfair play will be applied where appropriate.

E.1.c. POINTS
       1 point per stone placed in a construction zone.
       5 points per wall built.
       5 points per tower built
       20 points per built door

  E.2. I HAVE THE REAL ONE!

We have the Grail! And to show that we have the real one, we’re going to show it to the whole world, so that
everyone can see that it shines brightly!

E.2.a. DESCRIPTION AND LAYOUT OF THE PLAYING ELEMENTS
This action involves the Grail and the castle.

E.2.b. ACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS
 Actions:

       The team has to put its Grail on its throne rome during the preparation time.
       During the match, the team must raise their Grail as high as possible so that everyone can see it.

 Constraints:

       During the preparation, the Grail must be placed in the throne room floor, remaining entirely within the
       designated area and without being under the robot’s control.
       The Grail must comply with the same construction constraints as a SIMA, except for the minimum size,
       the Grail must be larger than a 60 mm side cube.
       During the match, the robot must pick up the Grail and place it on a support (stone, construction or robot).
       The number of points awarded depends on the altitude of the lowest point of the Grail at the end of the
       match.

           • Level 1 corresponds to one stone horizontally
           • Level 2 to two stones horizontally
           • Level 3 to three stones horizontally or one vertically
           • Level 4 is a drop on the robot’s roof (>30cm) , but free of any actuators and any fixing system
       The Grail is allowed to exceed the allowed height limit but only within construction zones and the castle
       boundaries, and without blocking any opposing beacons (for seniors). Please note that the actuators
       must respect the height limit at all times during the match.
       During all the match, the Grail must stay fully inside the castle area. It will be removed from the table the
       opposite case.
       It is forbidden to move the opposing team’s Grail.
       A Grail that is still controlled by an actuator at the end of the match will not be counted.

E.2.c. POINTS
       5 points if the Grail is present in the castle.
       5 points per Grail elevation level.

  E.3. THE RETURN OF THE KING
Once the construction of his castle was finished, the king returned to court to feast with his valiant knights.

E.3.a. DESCRIPTION AND LAYOUT OF THE PLAYING ELEMENTS
This action involves the robots and the castle courtyard (SIMA are excluded from this action).

E.3.b. ACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS
 Actions:
       At the end of the match the robots must be stopped in their throne room.

 Constraints:
       To be considered partially in the zone, the main robot must have a part of its vertical projection in the
       arrival area.
       To be considered completely in the zone, the main robot must have all of its vertical projection in the
       arrival area.

E.3.c. POINTS
       5 points if the team’s main robot is partially in its own valid area.
       10 points if the team’s main robot is completely in its own valid area.

  E.4. TO THE ATTACK!

You’ve learned that another robot claims to be King Arthur, and worse still, it claims to possess the "real" Grail,
they’re surely French! You can’t let this go on; it’s time to teach it a lesson... but be careful, you mustn’t use
anything meaner than a grown rabbit!

E.4.a. DESCRIPTION AND LAYOUT OF THE PLAYING ELEMENTS

This action involves the robot, the SIMAs, the cannonballs, and the opposing castle.

E.4.b. ACTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS

 Actions:

       The team can release one or more SIMAS, and ensure that they reach the moat of the opposing castle
       during the attack phase.
       SIMAS can also attack opposing SIMAS during the attack phase.
       The team can also bombard the opposing castle during the attack phase.

 Constraints:

       During preparation, the SIMAs will be placed in the starting area on the table (the stable) and must remain
       within the boundaries of the area. The colored lines on the vinyl and the edge of the table (on its 22mm
       thickness) adjacent to the area are part of the starting area.
       During preparation, the cannonballs will be loaded into the robot and the SIMA with a limit of 1 cannonball
       per SIMA.
       The attack phase is active between the 85th second and the end of the match; any subsequent actions
       performed outside of this phase will not be counted.
       If a SIMA moves out of the starting zone before the attack phase, it will be removed from the table and
       will no longer be considered to be in the match.
       A SIMA is validated as being in the zone if all or part of its vertical projection is within a moat of the
       opposing castle at the end of the match.
       A SIMA is considered to be attacking an opposing SIMA if one of its actuators touches an opposing SIMA
       at the end of the match. Caution! This actuator must have a distinct color from the rest of the SIMA, must
       not damage the opposing SIMA, and only this actuator is allowed to touch the opposing SIMA.
       The robot and the SIMA can launch the cannonballs into the opposing castle. A cannonball is considered
       to be in the zone if all or part of its vertical projection is within the opposing castle’s courtyard at the end of
       the match. It is forbidden to intentionally influence the position or trajectory of the opposing cannonballs.
       The balls are not subject to a height limitation during their flights.

E.4.c. POINTS

       5 points per moat occupied by the team
       5 points if at least one SIMA attacks an opposing SIMA.
       5 points if one cannonball present in the opposing castle.
       15 additionnal points if at least 3 cannonball are present in the opposing castle.
